# Fantasy Moneyball Policy (Sport-Agnostic)

Applies to all fantasy leagues (football, hockey, etc.). League-specific rules, rosters, and player intel live in each project's own League Profile doc. When the two conflict, the league doc wins — rules beat principles.

---

## 1. Research Discipline (do this before any recommendation)

- **Never trust stale knowledge.** Player teams, contracts, injuries, and depth charts change every offseason. Verify current status via web search before valuing any player.
- **Check primary sources:** official team sites, Spotrac / CapFriendly (real-world contracts), beat reporters, and the league platform itself (CBS/Yahoo/ESPN) for fantasy salaries and contract status.
- **Date-stamp everything.** A July plan is a draft — recheck injuries, camp battles, and signings right before the keeper deadline and again before the draft/auction.
- **Confirm the fantasy contract column, not just salary.** Keeper cost depends on contract status (expired, FINAL/option year, years remaining). Never finalize a keeper decision without it.
- **Verify projections against scoring settings.** Generic PPR/standard rankings can mislead in custom-scoring leagues (e.g., big-play bonuses, 2-QB rosters, goalie-heavy formats). Re-weight accordingly.

## 2. Keeper / Contract Economics

- **Surplus value is the only metric:** (projected auction/market price) minus (keeper cost). Keep the biggest gaps, not the biggest names.
- **Escalators compound.** A 1.5x-per-year escalator roughly doubles cost every two years — cheap young players are the only ones worth multi-year deals.
- **Multi-year contracts on young, ascending bargains only.** Lock in 2–3 years when the year-2/year-3 price is still below expected market. Model the bust case first: know the buyout cost before signing.
- **Never pay option-year premiums (e.g., 2x FINAL) for aging or unreliable veterans.** A one-year rental at double price almost never beats redrafting the position.
- **Age curves by position:** running backs and goalies decline fast; QBs, elite WRs, and defensemen age more gracefully. Don't lock long deals on fast-decline positions.
- **Do the buyout math before every cut.** Uncontracted players are free cuts; contracted players carry dead cap. Sometimes the discipline play is eating a small buyout to free budget.

## 3. Market Mapping (know the room, not just your roster)

- **Audit every rival roster.** Apply the keeper math to their players too: whoever CAN'T economically keep a star is releasing that star into the pool.
- **Predict the auction pool.** Stars with bloated salaries + unrostered elites = supply. A flooded position means discounts — don't win the first bidding war.
- **Rank rivals by cap health.** Teams with cheap keeper cores enter the auction rich and drive up prices; teams protecting expensive stars enter poor. Bid accordingly.

## 4. Auction / Draft Tactics

- **Nominate players you DON'T want early** — drain rivals' budgets on the shiny names.
- **Buy the glut, pay up for scarcity.** Overpay only at genuinely thin positions; at flooded positions, wait for the second or third tier of the same quality.
- **Set a budget per remaining slot** and track it live. Leaving money unspent is as bad as overspending.
- **Target post-injury and situation-change discounts:** players returning from major injury with strong recovery reports, coaching/scheme upgrades, or cleared depth charts are systematically underpriced.

## 5. Insurance & Handcuffs

- **Own the backup of an elite starter** (yours or one you plan to buy) when the backup is nearly free. It's cheap insurance and trade leverage against whoever owns the starter.
- **Cheap lottery tickets over expensive certainty** at the back of the roster.

## 6. Schedule Management

- **Stagger byes/off-weeks across your core** so no single week guts the lineup. Check before keeping AND before every auction buy.

## 7. Deadline Checklist (every season)

1. Pull current rosters + salaries + contract statuses from the league site.
2. Re-verify injuries/team changes for all keeper candidates (fresh search, not memory).
3. Recompute surplus value; finalize keepers and contracts.
4. Map rival keepers → predict auction pool → set target list and max bids.
5. Submit before the deadline. Never submit off a stale plan.
